"How much does a website cost?" is the first question every business owner asks, and the honest answer is always the same: **it depends on what you actually need**. You might get three quotes for the same idea with big gaps between them, and all three can make sense, because each one assumes a different scope of work.

This guide walks through the factors that drive the price, so you know exactly what you're paying for and can compare quotes on a clear basis.

## 1. Website Type: A Simple Site or a Full-Blown System?

The biggest cost difference comes from the nature of the project itself:

- **Landing page:** A single page that introduces a product or service and moves visitors toward one action, such as getting in touch or making a booking.
- **Company website:** Several pages (Home, Services, Work, About, Contact) with mostly static content.
- **Online store:** Products, a cart, checkout, shipping, and a dashboard to manage orders.
- **Custom system or platform:** Users with different permissions, data, reports, and workflows specific to your business — like a clinic management system or an e-learning platform.

The further down this list you go, the more screens, scenarios, and business logic you need, and the more the cost and timeline grow with them.

## 2. Design: Ready-Made Template or Custom Design?

A template is faster and cheaper, but it makes your site look like countless others. It also tends to come loaded with features you don't need while missing the ones you do.

Custom design starts with understanding your audience and the purpose of the site, then builds everything around that. This is where stages like these come in:

- Analyzing your users and their journey through the site.
- Mapping out the page structure (wireframes).
- Designing the final interfaces with a consistent visual identity.
- Designing the edge cases: errors, empty states, and mobile screens.

## 3. Brand Identity: Already in Place or Built from Scratch?

If you already have an approved logo, colors, and fonts, design can start right away. If you don't have an identity yet, building one becomes part of the job: the logo, color palette, typography, and guidelines for using them. That adds time, but it saves you from rework later.

## 4. Number of Pages and Content

Page count matters, but what matters more is **the number of distinct page templates**. Ten service pages that share one design are easier to build than five pages that each look different.

Also ask: who's writing the content? Clear marketing copy and ready-to-use images are a job in their own right — and late content is the most common reason website launches slip.

## 5. Features and Integrations

Every extra feature comes with its own cost:

- Contact or request forms that are saved and delivered to your inbox.
- User sign-in and accounts.
- A content dashboard so you can update the site without a developer.
- Payment gateways, SMS or WhatsApp messaging, and integrations with other systems.
- Multilingual support, such as Arabic and English.

## 6. Performance and Search Visibility

A fast website that search engines understand doesn't happen by accident. Image compression, structured data (Schema), a sitemap, and prerendered content are all technical work that shapes how you show up on Google and in the answers AI tools give.

## 7. After Launch

The real cost doesn't stop on launch day. Ask about:

- **Hosting and domain:** Who pays for them, and whose name are they registered under?
- **Maintenance and updates:** Bug fixes, library updates, and backups.
- **Ongoing development:** Adding features based on how people actually use the site.

## How to Compare Website Quotes

When several quotes land on your desk, don't just compare the final number. Compare:

| Question | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- |
| Which stages are included? | Are design, branding, and testing part of the quote? |
| How many revision rounds? | Open-ended revisions are a common source of disputes. |
| Who owns the code and designs? | They should be entirely yours after handover. |
| Does it work on mobile? | Most of your visitors will come from their phones. |
| What happens after launch? | Support, maintenance, and response times. |

## The Bottom Line

There's no fixed "website price" — there's a scope of work that gets priced. The best way to get an accurate quote is to explain the **goal of your project** and who it's for before talking about pages and features. Start with the minimum that achieves that goal, then build from there. That's the whole idea behind an [MVP](/en/blog/what-is-mvp).
